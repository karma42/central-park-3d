# Geographic references, permissions and uncertainty

The first-milestone model is a **procedural, approximate** recreation of the Bethesda Terrace and Fountain area. It uses no surveyed geometry, imagery, textures or third-party 3D assets: every shape is generated by code in `viewer/src/`. All dimensions below are working estimates from general knowledge, written down so they can be checked and corrected.

> **Verification status:** these facts and figures were **not** checked against the sources listed below during this work session; there was no web access. Treat every number as provisional until someone confirms it against a primary source.

## Coordinate frame

- Units are meters. The frame is aligned to the park grid: Manhattan's street grid, and so the park's long axis, runs about 29° east of true north. "Park-north" is used throughout.
- `+x` = park-east, `+y` = up, `-z` = park-north (toward the Lake).
- Origin = center of Bethesda Fountain at the level of the lower-terrace paving.

## Layout facts the model encodes

| Feature | What the model assumes | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Two-level terrace | Upper terrace about 5.5 m above the lower plaza. The upper level carries Terrace Drive (72nd Street Transverse) and the north end of the Mall | Medium: the relationship is well known, the height is estimated |
| Arcade | A passage under Terrace Drive linking the Mall's central staircase to the lower plaza, with arched openings and an encaustic (Minton) tile ceiling | High for the arrangement, low for dimensions and tile pattern (the pattern is a stylized placeholder) |
| Grand staircases | Two flights flanking the arcade, descending from the upper terrace to the plaza | Medium |
| Bethesda Fountain | Central basin about 29 m (~96 ft) across. Two-tier structure topped by the *Angel of the Waters* (Emma Stebbins, 1873), about 8 m (~26 ft) overall. Four cherub figures (Temperance, Purity, Health, Peace) around the column | Medium: basin and statue height need checking; figures are simplified |
| The Lake | Lies park-north of the plaza. The plaza's north edge meets the water, with a central landing | High for the arrangement, low for the shoreline shape (stylized) |
| The Mall | Straight promenade running park-south from the terrace, lined by four rows of American elms | High for the arrangement; width, row spacing and tree count are estimates |
| Surrounding paths | Lakeside walks east (toward the boathouse) and west (toward Bow Bridge), plus links up to Terrace Drive | Low: illustrative alignments only |
| Skyline context | Central Park West twin-towered buildings (San Remo, Majestic, Century) and Billionaires' Row towers to the south | Low: positions and heights are rough, for orientation only |

## Sources to verify against (not yet consulted)

The work should check its numbers against these before any accuracy claim. Licensing must be confirmed before reusing any data from them; nothing from them is in the repository today.

1. **NYC Parks**: official Central Park / Bethesda Terrace pages (history, descriptions).
2. **Central Park Conservancy**: Bethesda Terrace and Arcade pages, and the restoration histories of the Minton tile ceiling and fountain.
3. **NYC Open Data / NYC Planimetrics**: building footprints, park paths and hydrography (published openly; check the dataset terms).
4. **OpenStreetMap**: paths, water bodies and tree points around Bethesda Terrace (ODbL; requires attribution and share-alike for derived databases).
5. **USGS 3DEP / NYC topobathymetric LiDAR**: terrain and elevation (generally public domain; confirm per dataset).
6. **Historic American Buildings Survey (HABS)** drawings in the Library of Congress, if available for the terrace: measured drawings (typically public domain).

## Known simplifications

- Terrain is a smooth analytic function, not elevation data. The Lake, hills and shoreline are stylized.
- The terrace is built from boxes. There is no carved ornament, and the arches are segmental approximations.
- Tree positions are seeded random placements, not the park's tree inventory.
- Lighting is a fixed sunny afternoon.
